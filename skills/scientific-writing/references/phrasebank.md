# Phrasebank

Attested connectives and constructions, grouped by the job they do. Frequencies
are per 10,000 words in the Tier 1 papers (see `corpus_and_evidence.md`). Use
this to check whether a phrase belongs, not to shop for variety — repetition of
the *same* connective across a paper is normal and correct here.

---

## Load-bearing emphasis (budget: 2–3 per section)

These point at the design feature or analysis property that licenses an
inference. If the sentence survives deleting the adverb with no loss of meaning,
delete the adverb.

| Phrase | Rate | Attested use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Crucially,` | 1–3 | "Crucially, the payoffs of the safe and risky options were presented sequentially." |
| `Critically,` | 1–2 | "Critically, this model disentangles choice consistency from preference, avoiding the confounds inherent in raw choice proportions." |
| `Importantly,` | 1 | "Importantly, the decoded neural uncertainty (s.d. of the decoded posterior) was significantly correlated with the error of the decoder." |
| `Notably,` | 1 | "Notably, while at the group level, the subjective prior for risky payoffs did not have a higher mean…" |
| `Notably, one study has already shown that…` | — | Used to introduce the single closest piece of prior work before saying what it left open. |

## Workaday argument connectives (use freely)

| Phrase | Rate | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Thus,` | 6–8 | Draws the consequence. The most frequent connective in the corpus by a wide margin. |
| `However,` | 6–7 | The turn. Also used mid-sentence: "Empirical data reveal, however, that…" |
| `Moreover,` | 3–4 | Adds a second, independent support. |
| `Furthermore,` | 1–3 | Same, slightly heavier. |
| `Specifically,` | 0–4 | Narrows from claim to instance. |
| `Indeed,` | 1–2 | Confirms with an independent observation. |
| `Conversely,` | — | Symmetric contrast, usually within a predicted pattern. |
| `In line with …` | 1–3 | Prediction met, or agreement with prior work. |
| `By contrast,` | — | Switches to the rival literature. |
| `Relatedly,` | — | Moves from lab to life in the opening paragraph. |

## Prediction-confirmation flags

- "In line with our predictions, …"
- "In line with our expectations, …"
- "In line with a perceptual account of risky choice, …"
- "In line with a CT effect for the first option, …"
- "In line with earlier work (…), …"
- "These results align with the psychophysical modelling results: …"
- "This analysis confirmed that …"
- "Posterior predictive plots confirmed that our model accurately predicts the observed empirical patterns at the group level."

## Technical caveats — `Note that …`

About three per paper. For a point a careless reader would miss.

- "Note that this model A is equivalent to a standard probit psychophysical model."
- "Note that we refrained from analyzing raw choice proportions since they cannot be interpreted cleanly in isolation."
- "Note that the psychophysical model we use here is a measurement model and thus remains agnostic to the precise underlying mechanisms."
- "Note that the point-of-indifference of the probit model is a function of both the intercept δ and slope parameter γ."
- "Note that, on average, such biases are beneficial because they reduce the average perceptual error."
- "Note that these six parameters can be estimated in our experimental paradigm because the noise of the evidence is experimentally manipulated by order, orthogonal to the riskiness of the option."
- "Note that we chose to stimulate right IPS based on literature in numerical cognition that suggest right-lateralization of the ANS."

The last two show the two dominant flavours: *why this is identifiable* and *why
this choice was made*.

## Summary flags (one per Results subsection)

- "In sum, …"
- "In summary, …"
- "In conclusion, …" (used at the end of a Results subsection as well as the paper)
- "Taking stock, the key finding of this additional experiment is that …"
- "Together, these results …"
- "All in all, we demonstrate that …"
- "Thus, …" (for a short subsection)

## Forward transitions

- "We now turn to more mechanistic computational models of choice to better understand this empirical pattern."
- "Having established that …, we addressed the final question of our study: …"
- "In the following, we will first describe …"
- "To be able to test this hypothesis, we first fitted …"
- "Before linking neural uncertainty to behaviour, we first assessed the acuity of the decoder."
- "To address these questions, we introduce …"
- "After successfully fitting the nPRF model, we used …"
- "To account for the results more mechanistically, we also fitted …"

## Previewing results

- "Previewing our results, stimulation reduced the fidelity of parietal tuning to the presented payoff magnitudes."
- "Here, we show that rapid changes in risk attitudes arise from Bayesian inference on noisy parietal magnitude representations."
- "Together, these results provide critical insights into how …"

## Self-anticipated objections

Openers, all followed by a genuine concession before the answer:

- "One question about our model might be whether it is unnecessarily complex, as …"
- "One might argue that this choice context may induce effects in a purely 'low-level perceptual' manner that should be absent for …"
- "One might wonder, however, if the observed effects … might be specific to …"
- "A related critique of Bayesian theories, and potentially the PMCM, is that …"
- "Some have argued that this may make Bayesian models of cognition overly flexible (but see ref. X)."
- "To address these concerns, we conducted an additional experiment with …"
- "To make sure that our model comparison approach was sufficiently powerful, we also simulated …"
- "To make sure we found an effect of neural uncertainty over and above any order- and magnitude-effects, we performed a median split …"

Concession-then-bound constructions:

- "Although these theories alleviate the problem of choice inconsistency, they do not specifically address the question of where this randomness comes from."
- "While this evidently does not prove that X, it still suggests that Y."
- "Having said that, future work should validate whether …"
- "Nevertheless, the PMCM does give us an increased understanding of …"
- "Clearly, the precise role of parietal cortex in economic choice remains to be fully elucidated and is likely to depend on …"
- "While much work remains, our findings show how …"

## Rhetorical questions

Always load-bearing, and answered by a subsequent subsection.

- "Could the fidelity and distortions of these parietal neural representations of the key numerical determinants of a choice problem be driving some of the economic choices we make?"
- "Where do these apparent fluctuations in risk attitudes come from?"
- "Can we mechanistically account for the influence of context on economic choice, by studying how it alters the perception of the choice-relevant information?"
- "Might the preference for gambling be explained as a misperception of the potential payoffs?"
- "Can they originate from endogenous fluctuations of neural noise, in analogy to how such fluctuations affect perceptual decisions about low-level visual features?"

## Gap statements

- "However, this correlational relationship leaves open the question whether …"
- "Whether and how this code adapts … remains unknown."
- "Thus, a precise, mechanistic characterization of the causal role of the parietal ANS in risky choice is lacking."
- "However, studies on this topic have so far been exclusively correlational, raising the critical question of whether …"
- "Without causal evidence, it remains possible that a factor outside the parietal cortex drives both behavior and the precision of parietal magnitude representations."
- "However, this study did not look into the neural effects of the stimulation, nor did it investigate whether …"

## Hedges — for interpretations only

*suggests* · *may* · *might* · *could* · *appears to* · *seems* · *in line with* ·
*broadly consistent with* · *at least partly* · *at least under some conditions* ·
*can be rooted in* · *one promising working hypothesis could be that* ·
*we believe these results might reflect* · *this raises the possibility that*

Never applied to a result that the statistics support. "We found a very robust
decrease in risk aversion after cTBS" takes no hedge.

## Scope qualifiers worth stealing

- "at least in the small-stake laboratory experiments widely used in the field"
- "at least when magnitudes are involved"
- "originate, at least partly, from perceptual neural processing in parietal cortex"
- "is likely to depend on the specifics of the choice task, as well as the phase of decision-making during which one monitors parietal activity"

## Phrases absent from the corpus

Do not introduce: *delve*, *tapestry*, *landscape*, *realm*, *multifaceted*,
*holistic*, *pivotal*, *cutting-edge*, *paradigm shift*, *nuanced*,
*it is important to note that*, *it is well known that*, *further research is
needed*, and exclamation marks.

Conversely, these **do** occur and should not be pruned as "AI words":
*elucidate*, *well-known*, *robustly*, *novel*, *showcase*, *shed light*,
*dovetail*, *underscore*, *profoundly*, *substantially*, *simply*.
